What it is

  • A small chat model from Nirca, a project that trains models from scratch on a Mac
  • Made to be good company: a buddy who helps you think things through
  • 109 million weights, a 33 MB download
  • Runs on your own GPU with WebGPU: nothing you type leaves the page
It's an experiment in how far careful training takes a small model, not a product. It's small and it says odd things; that's part of the point.

The model in one picture

Three ideas: it reads raw bytes, not words; one block is reused in a loop and the model chooses how many times (adaptive halting); and the weights are ternary, which is why the whole model is 33 MB.

How it's trained

Two kinds of data: open chat data (SmolTalk-2, chats up to 2,048 bytes) and synthetic chats, where two open models play a person and Nirca Mini. A leak sweep drops anything that overlaps the test sets. The checkpoint on the page is step 62,377; about 7% of what it read in that stretch was synthetic.

The charter

“Your job is to be good company: a friendly companion, and above all a buddy who helps people think things through.”

Values, in order: honesty, kindness, clarity, curiosity, respect.

“An honest ‘I’m not sure’ beats a confident, kind-sounding answer.”

The charter steers the synthetic chats: the model playing Nirca Mini is given it. The page sends no system prompt; whatever shows of it, the model learned. The full text is on the overview page.
